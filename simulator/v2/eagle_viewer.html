Hawk-Eagle Drone

A flapping-wing drone built to a Mountain Hawk-Eagle's size and dressed as one. It flies in, flares onto a snag, folds its wings along its back, scans with its head, opens up and takes off again. Drone numbers are measured off the model in this page. * Golden eagles flap at 2.2–2.8 Hz; the lighter hawk-eagle sits near 3 Hz. Getting the wingbeat right matters as much as the paint.

How the wing folds

  1. Sweep. The upper arm swings back 86° at the shoulder.
  2. Roll. The wing plane rolls 54°, so the folded wing lies over the back and flank.
  3. Z-fold. Elbow closes to 168°, wrist to 166°. The primaries slide under the secondaries and their tips end over the tail.
  • per wing10 primaries (6 fingered), 13 secondaries and tertials, 3 humerals, coverts on each
  • tail12 feathers that fan to ±48° for the landing flare
  • rigone skinned mesh per bird

The bare airframe hangs carbon lamellae on the same joints, so it folds the same way under the disguise.

Reference airframe

  • Perches upright inside the canopy and scans with quick head turns, as raptors do.
  • Lands with a flare: tail fanned, feet forward, two balancing beats, then folds.
  • Motor off on the perch, so on thermal it sits near air temperature.
